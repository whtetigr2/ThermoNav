## What was built

**`link_health.py`** — two pieces, deliberately separated by an observability boundary:

1. **`LinkChannel` (the wire, hidden state lives here only).** GCS-origin packets (heartbeat / operator intent / override) at 10 Hz. Each packet draws a latency and a loss outcome from the current *condition*:
   - Drivers: `nominal` (always good), `scripted` (condition schedule — deterministic scenario levers), `markov` (two-state Gilbert–Elliott good/degraded chain + Poisson dropout bursts — for sweeps).
   - Conditions: `good` (25±8 ms, 0.5 % loss), `degraded` (180±60 ms, 25 % loss), `dropout` (100 % loss).
   - Latency is real in the mechanics: a packet enters a queue with `arrival = send + latency` and is only delivered when the wire advances past that time. Loss is real: dropped packets never arrive.
2. **`LinkHealthTracker` (the receiver).** Sees **only** what a real receiver can report: delivered-packet arrival times, per-packet latency (packet timestamps), and expected-vs-delivered counts (sequence numbers) in a trailing 2 s window. It never receives the channel condition. Output report: `state` (HEALTHY / DEGRADED / LOST), `score`, `time_in_state_s`, `silence_s`, `window_loss_frac`, `latency_ewma_ms`, delivered/expected counts.
   - DEGRADED if trailing loss ≥ 20 % **or** latency EWMA ≥ 150 ms, with hysteresis exits (loss < 10 % and EWMA < 100 ms).
   - LOST if silence ≥ 5.0 s. First packet after LOST lands in DEGRADED (recovering), not straight to HEALTHY.

**`escalation.py` (policy wiring).** `EscalationPolicy.evaluate()` gains an optional `link_report` argument. Without it, behavior is bit-for-bit the old stub (all existing tests pass unmodified). With it:
- two **leaky-bucket** timers accumulate from the report sequence (the policy never sees the channel). One semantics for both buckets (revised after red-team round 1, F1/F2 — see the fix log): a bucket accumulates at 1×dt while its bad state (or a worse one) is reported, decays at 0.5×dt on any better report, floor 0, capped at its firing threshold. The round-1 semantics (DEGRADED timer reset on a single HEALTHY report; LOST timer held across DEGRADED gaps) were neither sustained nor cumulative-honest and are gone — the word "sustained" is not used for link rows anywhere in code or this report;
- rows: **LOST declared → HOVER** immediately; **LOST-evidence bucket ≥ 10 s → RTH**; **DEGRADED-evidence bucket ≥ 5 s → HOVER**. Under a continuous bad state the buckets fill in exactly 10 s / 5 s; under a 50 %-duty flapping link they fill in ~2× that — and they do fill (round-1's flap defeat is regression-tested). **Commit semantics (revised after red-team round 2, N1/N2 — full disposition below):** a bucket reaching its threshold makes the action *desired*; the 10 s hold window that follows is now **leaky, not brittle**. Hold progress accumulates at 1×dt while the harsh desire persists and *leaks* at 3.5×dt while it is absent, so a one-report dip that drops the (capped) bucket below threshold dents the window instead of cancelling it — the fast path's break-even is ~78 % desire duty across the window horizon. A second integrator, **net LOST exposure** (+1×dt on LOST reports, *hold* on DEGRADED, −1×dt on HEALTHY, capped at its threshold), **latches** the RTH desire at 75 s, so links whose LOST runs are too broken for the fast path still commit in bounded time once their cumulative blackout-minus-recovery balance crosses 75 s, and the desire then survives DEGRADED dips until HEALTHY reports drain the exposure. The latch does **not** fire for every broken link, however: because the exposure is capped at 75 s, a LOST/HEALTHY shape whose HEALTHY gaps drain exposure equilibrates below the latch — cycle-start exposure equilibrates at 75 − (HEALTHY gap), so the 55 %-duty shape (11 s LOST / 9 s HEALTHY) and the 60 %-duty shape **never commit at any horizon (measured to 2400 s)**, even though the 55 % shape accumulates +2 s net per 20 s cycle at first. The latch fires as documented when the gaps are DEGRADED (which holds exposure): LOST 1 s / DEGRADED 9 s commits at 750.5 s (measured, round 3). Symmetrically, **release of a committed RTH/LAND now requires sustained recovery evidence**: both link buckets fully drained to zero — ~20 s of sustained HEALTHY reports from FULL buckets (the fast-path commit case); latch commits release in 10–15 s because their buckets are part-drained at commit (measured, round 3: 15.0 s for the 5 s/5 s straddle, 11.0 s for 9 s/9 s, 10.0 s when release is governed by the DEGRADED bucket alone) — and the release steps down to HOVER, never straight to mission resumption. One DEGRADED report (all a single stray packet manufactures) changes nothing in either direction. Padded brief dropouts (LOST 4 s / DEGRADED 4 s, LOST 3 s / HEALTHY 3 s, LOST 8 s / DEGRADED 2 s) still never commit *in their tested sequences* (desire duty ~75 % < the 78 % fast-path break-even; net exposure 48 s < the 75 s latch) — sustained for minutes they are no longer brief, and the latch commits them (8 s/2 s at 100.5 s, 4 s/4 s at 155.5 s; measured, documented in the round-2 disposition). Continuous LOST commits RTH at report 19–20 at mission rate (10 s bucket + hold window), regression-tested; the shortest single committing LOST episode is unchanged at 19.5 s (dt 0.5) / 19.0 s (dt 1.0). The DEGRADED→HOVER row needs no window (HOVER engages immediately), so flapping escalates to HOVER and stays responsive there;
- link rows merge with perception rows by the existing most-severe-wins rule, pass through the existing hold-first override window, and RTH passes through the existing pose-trust gate (closed → land-in-place, logged `rth_gated_land_in_place`; link escalations also logged as `link_escalation`);
- genuine recovery de-escalates: it drains a pending RTH's hold progress to zero (round-2 leaky semantics — a dip dents it, sustained recovery clears it) and, once a commit's release evidence is met, steps the action down (tested);
- `"link"` leaves `missing_triggers` when a report is supplied; battery and nav-health remain MISSING.

**`mission_dynamics_v5.py` (mission wiring).** `cfg["link"]` spec dict enables the layer. Per outer step the loop sends 10 packets (one per 0.1 s tick), advances the wire, and feeds the tracker report to the policy. Operator override is no longer a free boolean: `cfg["link_operator_override_window"]` marks override packets *sent* by the GCS; the policy sees the override **only if the channel delivers the packet** — a lost link physically cannot cancel a pending escalation. Manifest row gains 14 link columns (final state, per-state step counts, max silence, packets sent/dropped/delivered, max latency, delivered-override steps, link-escalation event count).

## Timers: grounded vs still design choices

- **Grounded (one):** the **LOST declaration at 5.0 s of silence** — cited autopilot practice (ArduPilot default). This is the dropout-duration threshold the task asked whether the model could ground. It lives in the tracker, not the policy.
- **Still design choices:** the ladder's pre-existing 5 s / 30 s perception timers and 10 s hold window (unchanged); the link bucket thresholds (5 s / 10 s) and leak rate (0.5×) (labeled `[design]` in code); all DEGRADED classification thresholds; every channel condition parameter. None of these are measured — there is no link hardware and no flight data in this lab.
- **Citation nuance (red-team F6):** the 5.0 s LOST value is cited; the *semantics* are imported selectively — ArduPilot keeps its GCS failsafe inactive if no GCS was ever connected, while this tracker declares LOST 5 s after start with zero packets ever received. In-sim the GCS always sends, so the divergence is latent. Documented here and in `link_health.py`; behavior unchanged.

## Scope choices (honest scoping)

- **Decider kernel unchanged.** The 12-spin Z1-legal decider maps the *perception* trigger table; link rows are host-policy rows, the same status battery/nav-health slots always had. Extending the kernel to link inputs is a separate LEGO (it would change the 128-combo grid and the kernel's Z1 stats for a hand-designed field map — not needed for the ladder to consume link health, which it now does).
- **What the link carries in this stack:** GCS heartbeat/intent/override uplink. The autonomy (S2/D5) is onboard and correctly does *not* stop for link loss — the failsafe ladder is the response, which is the industry pattern the ladder mirrors. Downlink telemetry loss is not separately modeled (the tracker is uplink-receiver-side; a one-way-link model is future work).
- **Latency does not delay onboard control** (it isn't on that path in reality either). Latency delays *operator intent/override delivery* and stretches silence/loss statistics — both wired, both tested.

## Known limits

1. All channel parameters are assumed/cited, none measured. The first real telemetry log (any radio) should replace the `[ASSUMED]` rows — the table is structured for that swap.
2. Scripted scenarios are deterministic levers, not evidence about real dropout distributions; the Markov driver exists for sweeps but its parameters are equally assumed.
3. Link + perception simultaneous escalation is merged by most-severe-wins but was not mission-tested in combination (policy-level merge is covered by the pre-existing most-severe-wins tests plus link-row unit tests).
4. Override delivery is modeled; a dedicated mission with an override window racing a dropout is future work (unit-level: override is just a delivered payload flag; mission-level race not run).
5. DISARM remains reachable only via the existing terminal path (landed → mission end); the link rows never emit DISARM directly — same as the perception rows.
6. **Land actuation under link loss is UNTESTED at mission level** (red-team F4). The ladder reaches the gated LAND *action*; descent is blocked by the pre-existing below-clear guard, which is structurally unsatisfiable from altitude (floor layer pinned occupied by the boundary prior; steep below-column unobserved — full analysis in the F4 disposition). Until that guard is revisited in its own safety-reviewed change, a lost-link vehicle in this stack holds at hover rather than landing. Fixing the guard is follow-up work, explicitly not part of this build.
